About the BMI calculator

Body mass index (BMI) compares your weight with your height. It is a quick way to see whether your weight is in a healthy range. Enter your height and weight in metric or imperial units to see your BMI, your category and the healthy weight range for your height.

How BMI is worked out

BMI = weight in kg ÷ (height in metres)². For example, 70 kg and 1.70 m gives 70 ÷ 2.89 = 24.2. The international WHO categories are: under 18.5 underweight, 18.5 to 24.9 healthy, 25 to 29.9 overweight and 30 or more obesity. The WHO also suggests lower cut-offs for Asian populations, because health risks such as diabetes start at lower BMI: 23 for overweight and 27.5 for obesity. Choose “WHO Asian” if that applies to you.

Limits of BMI

BMI doesn't tell muscle from fat, so very muscular people can score high, and older adults can score low while carrying extra fat. Waist size matters too: a large waist increases health risk at any BMI. For children and teenagers, BMI must be compared with growth charts by age and sex. Use the result as a starting point and talk to a doctor about your own health.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?

18.5 to 24.9 on the international scale, or 18.5 to 22.9 on the Asian scale.

Why is there an Asian scale?

People of South and East Asian origin tend to develop weight-related illness at lower BMI.

Does it work for children?

Not directly; children need age-based growth charts.

Is my data saved?

No.
