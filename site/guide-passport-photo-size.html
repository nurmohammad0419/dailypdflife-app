A rejected photo is one of the most common reasons a passport, visa or residency application is delayed. The rules are strict about size, background, head position and lighting, and they differ from country to country. The good news is that with a phone camera, a plain wall and the free Passport photo maker you can make a photo that meets the rules and print it for very little money.

Rules change, and different offices sometimes ask for slightly different sizes, so always confirm with the official website or the embassy before printing. If a size isn't in the list, use Custom size in the tool.

How to take the photo

  1. Stand in front of a plain, light wall, about half a metre away from it so there's no shadow behind you.
  2. Face a window or a bright light. Light from the front makes the face even; light from behind makes it dark.
  3. Ask someone else to take it from about 1.5 metres away, with the phone at your eye level. Selfies make the nose look bigger and are often rejected.
  4. Look straight at the camera with a neutral face, mouth closed, eyes open, and both ears and the full face visible. Remove glasses unless the rules allow them. Religious head coverings are normally allowed if the face from chin to forehead is visible.
  5. Take several shots and pick the sharpest.

How to make the photo

  1. Open the Passport photo maker and choose your photo.
  2. Select the size for your country.
  3. Drag and zoom until your face fits the red oval: the top of the head near the top line and the chin near the bottom.
  4. Tap Remove background and choose white or the colour your country asks for.
  5. Adjust brightness and contrast only slightly; heavy editing can get a photo rejected.
  6. Download a single JPG for online forms, or a 4×6 print sheet to print at a photo shop. One 4×6 print gives you several copies.

Uploading to an online portal

Many portals have a file size limit, often between 50 KB and 240 KB, and sometimes a pixel size such as 600 × 600. If your photo is too big, use Resize image to set the exact pixels and Compress image to reduce the file size. Keep the quality around 80% so the face stays clear.

Reasons photos get rejected

  • Shadows on the face or on the background.
  • Head too small or too large in the frame, or tilted.
  • Blurry, grainy or over-edited photo.
  • Smiling with teeth, closed eyes or hair covering the eyes.
  • Wrong background colour or a background with patterns.
  • Photo older than six months, or no longer looks like you.
