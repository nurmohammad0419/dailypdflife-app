Most banks in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bangladesh and India now email monthly statements, credit card statements and salary certificates as password-protected PDF files. That is good for security, but it becomes a problem the moment you need to use the file: visa portals, embassies, loan applications and landlords often reject locked PDFs, and typing a long password every time you open the file gets tiring. This guide explains how to find the password, remove it safely and keep an unlocked copy.

Step 1: Find the password

A bank does not choose a random password. It builds one from information it already has about you and explains the pattern in the email or SMS that came with the statement. Read that message carefully, including the small print at the bottom. Common patterns are:

  • The first four letters of your name in capitals plus your date of birth, for example AHME0105 for Ahmed born on 1 May.
  • Your date of birth in DDMMYYYY format.
  • The last four or six digits of your account number, card number, mobile number or national ID / Iqama number.
  • A combination of two of these, such as the last four digits of your mobile followed by your birth year.

Passwords are case sensitive, so ahme and AHME are different. If you still cannot work it out, call the bank or check its website's FAQ for “e-statement password”. No tool can legally or practically guess a strong PDF password for you.

Step 2: Remove the password

  1. Open the Unlock PDF tool on your phone or computer.
  2. Choose the locked statement.
  3. Type the password and tap Unlock PDF.
  4. Save the unlocked copy. Give it a clear name such as Statement-2026-10-unlocked.pdf.

The unlocked file is identical to the original: same pages, same text, same quality. Only the encryption is removed. Because DailyPdfLife works inside your browser, the statement and the password are never uploaded to any server, which matters for a document that shows your balance and transactions.

Step 3: Put several months into one file

Visa and loan applications usually ask for the last three or six months of statements as one document. Unlock each month first, then open Merge PDF and pick the files in date order. If the combined file is too large for the portal, run it through Compress PDF on the Medium setting.

Step 4 (optional): Get the transactions into Excel

If you want to check your spending or total your salary credits, open the unlocked statement in PDF to Excel and choose “All pages in one sheet”. You get a spreadsheet of transactions you can sort, filter and add up.

Keep your documents safe

An unlocked statement can be read by anyone who gets hold of it. Store it in a private folder, don't leave it in shared WhatsApp groups, and delete copies you no longer need. If you must email it to someone, consider locking it again with your own password using Protect PDF and sending the password separately by SMS. When sharing a statement only as proof of address or salary, you can hide unrelated transactions with Redact PDF.

Common problems

“Wrong password” even though I'm sure it's right. Check capital letters, the date format (DDMMYYYY or DDMMYY) and whether there should be no spaces. Some banks changed their pattern recently, so look at the email for that exact statement.

The PDF opens without a password but I can't print or copy. That file has only restrictions, not an opening password. Unlock PDF removes those restrictions too.

The portal still rejects the file. Check the size limit and the format. Some portals require a file under 2 MB; compress it, then try again.
