Recruiters in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar and the rest of the Gulf often receive hundreds of CVs for one job and spend less than a minute on each. A clear CV that gives them the details they care about, in the order they expect, makes a real difference. Gulf CVs also differ from UK or US CVs in a few ways, mainly in personal details. Here is a section-by-section guide you can follow in the free CV maker.

1. Header and personal details

Put your full name as it appears in your passport, your job title (for example “Mechanical Site Engineer – MEP”), mobile number with country code, email and city. Gulf employers commonly expect a few more details than Western ones:

  • Nationality and, if you are already in the country, your visa or Iqama status, for example “Iqama transferable” or “Available on visit visa”.
  • Driving licence, especially a Saudi, UAE or GCC licence, which matters for field and sales roles.
  • Notice period or availability date.
  • A professional photo is common and often expected. Use a plain background and business clothes; the Passport photo tool can clean up the background.

Date of birth and marital status are still often included in the region, but they are optional. Never include your passport or Iqama number on a CV you send to many companies.

2. Profile

Three or four lines that say who you are, your years of experience, your main area and what you're looking for. Example: “MEP engineer with 8 years in hospital and residential projects in Saudi Arabia. Experienced in plumbing and fire-fighting design review, site supervision and handover. Saudi Council of Engineers registered.”

3. Work experience

List jobs from newest to oldest with job title, company, city and dates. Under each, write two to five bullet points that show results, not just duties. Compare:

  • Weak: “Responsible for site supervision.”
  • Strong: “Supervised 25 technicians on a 300-bed hospital project; handed over on schedule with zero lost-time incidents.”

Numbers such as project size, budget, team size, sales targets or time saved make your experience easy to judge. Mention well-known clients or projects; recognisable names help in the Gulf.

4. Education and certificates

Give your highest qualification, the institution and the year. Professional registrations and certificates often matter as much as degrees: Saudi Council of Engineers or UAE engineering registration, NEBOSH or OSHA safety, PMP, accounting qualifications, nursing and medical licences (often with DataFlow verification). List them clearly with the year.

5. Skills and languages

List specific skills: software (AutoCAD, Revit, SAP, Excel, Tally), equipment and technical areas. For languages, English and Arabic are especially valued; give an honest level for each, plus your other languages such as Bangla, Urdu, Hindi or Tagalog, which can help in teams and customer roles.

6. Length, format and file

  • Keep it to two pages, or one if you have under five years' experience.
  • Use a simple layout. Many companies use applicant tracking systems that read text, so avoid putting important words inside images.
  • Use words from the job advert, such as the exact job title and required skills, where they truthfully describe you.
  • Send it as a PDF named clearly, for example Ahmed-Rahman-MEP-Engineer-CV.pdf.

7. Add a short cover letter

A brief cover letter or email that names the job and gives two reasons you fit helps you stand out. The Cover letter maker gives you a starting draft to edit. Finally, check spelling, phone number and email twice; a single wrong digit can cost you an interview.
