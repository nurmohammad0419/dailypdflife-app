“File size must not exceed 1 MB.” Anyone who has applied for a visa, a job, a university place or a government service online has seen a message like this. Phone cameras now take 12 to 50 megapixel photos, so a PDF made from a few photographed pages can easily be 10 or 20 MB. Here is how to bring it down, starting with the quickest fix.

1. Compress the PDF

Open Compress PDF, choose your file and start with Medium. This lowers the resolution and quality of the images inside the PDF to a level that still looks sharp on screen and prints well, and it usually cuts scanned documents by 60 to 90 percent. If the file is still too big, try Heavy. Check the result by zooming in on small text to make sure it's still readable.

2. Remove pages you don't need

Every page adds size. Blank pages, cover pages, adverts and terms-and-conditions pages are often not required. Use Remove pages to delete them, then compress. If the portal asks for a single document, send only what they asked for.

3. Split a large file across several uploads

Some portals allow several files but limit each one. Use Split PDF with page ranges, for example pages 1-5 in one file and 6-10 in another.

4. Make it smaller from the start

  • Use Scan to PDF instead of plain photos. Scan to PDF crops the page and the “Document” look turns it into clean black and white, which is far smaller than a colour photo of paper on a table.
  • Choose A4 page size rather than “same as photo”, so huge photos are scaled to a normal page.
  • Resize photos before turning them into a PDF. A width of 1600 pixels is plenty for a document page. Use Resize image, then JPG to PDF.
  • Export from Word or Excel directly to PDF rather than printing and scanning. A text PDF of 10 pages is often under 200 KB.

Why some PDFs don't shrink

A PDF that is mostly text, such as a contract exported from Word, is already compact; there are no big images to compress. Large embedded fonts can also add a few hundred kilobytes. In that case, removing pages or splitting the file are the best options.

Keep your files private

Many free compression sites upload your documents to their servers. DailyPdfLife compresses in your browser, so passports, bank statements and certificates never leave your device.
