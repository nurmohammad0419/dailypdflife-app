Car dealers and finance companies in the Gulf and South Asia often advertise a low “flat rate”, such as 3.5% or 4%. Bank home finance and many personal loans use a “reducing balance” rate instead. The two numbers look similar but are calculated differently, and the difference in what you actually pay can be thousands. This guide explains both with simple examples you can check in the Loan & EMI calculator.

Flat rate

With a flat rate, profit (interest) is calculated on the full original amount for every year of the loan, even though you are paying it back month by month.

Example: SAR 100,000 for 5 years at 4% flat.

  • Profit per year: 100,000 × 4% = SAR 4,000.
  • Total profit over 5 years: SAR 20,000.
  • Total to pay: SAR 120,000, or SAR 2,000 a month for 60 months.

Reducing balance

With a reducing balance rate, each month's profit is calculated only on what you still owe. As you pay off the loan, the profit part of each payment gets smaller.

Same example: SAR 100,000 for 5 years at 4% reducing.

  • Monthly payment: about SAR 1,842.
  • Total profit: about SAR 10,500.
  • Total to pay: about SAR 110,500.

So at the same 4%, the flat-rate loan costs roughly SAR 9,500 more, nearly double the profit.

Converting a flat rate

As a rough rule, a flat rate is equal to a reducing rate of about 1.8 to 1.9 times as much for typical 3 to 5 year loans. A 4% flat rate over 5 years works out to about a 7.4% reducing rate. Enter the offer in the loan calculator with “Flat rate”, note the monthly payment, then switch to “Reducing balance” and raise the rate until the payment matches. That's the true comparable rate.

How to compare offers

  1. Ask each lender whether the rate is flat or reducing, and ask for the APR (annual percentage rate), which includes fees.
  2. Add the admin or processing fee, insurance and any compulsory extras to the total.
  3. Compare the total amount you will pay, not only the monthly instalment. A longer period gives a smaller instalment but a bigger total.
  4. Check the early settlement terms: how much do you save if you pay off early?

A larger down payment

Every riyal or taka you pay upfront is a riyal you don't pay profit on. Try entering different down payments in the calculator to see the effect on both the monthly payment and the total cost.

This article is general information, not financial advice. Banks' actual terms, fees and calculation methods vary; always read the official offer and the key facts statement before signing.
