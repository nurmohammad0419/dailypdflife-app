Value Added Tax (VAT) in Saudi Arabia has been 15% since July 2020. Shop prices are normally shown including VAT, while business quotations are often written before VAT. Mixing the two up is an easy way to lose money on a deal, so it helps to know the two basic calculations. You can always use the Saudi VAT calculator, but here is what it does.

Adding 15% VAT to a price

Multiply the price before VAT by 1.15.

  • SAR 1,000 before VAT → VAT is 1,000 × 0.15 = SAR 150 → total SAR 1,150.
  • SAR 2,480 before VAT → 2,480 × 1.15 = SAR 2,852.

Removing VAT from a VAT-inclusive total

Divide the total by 1.15. Don't subtract 15% of the total: that gives the wrong answer.

  • SAR 1,150 including VAT → 1,150 ÷ 1.15 = SAR 1,000 before VAT, and VAT is SAR 150.
  • SAR 575 including VAT → 575 ÷ 1.15 = SAR 500, VAT SAR 75.

The common mistake: taking 15% off 1,150 gives 977.50, which is SAR 22.50 too low. The VAT inside a total is always 15/115 of it, which is about 13.04%.

Checking an invoice

  1. Add up the line items before VAT.
  2. Subtract any discount before calculating VAT; VAT is charged on the discounted amount.
  3. Multiply the result by 0.15 to get the VAT and add it to get the total.
  4. A tax invoice from a VAT-registered business should show the seller's VAT number, the date, the amount before VAT, the VAT amount and the total.

Small businesses and freelancers

Businesses must register for VAT with ZATCA once taxable sales pass the mandatory threshold (SAR 375,000 a year at the time of writing), and may register voluntarily above a lower threshold. Registered businesses must also follow the e-invoicing (Fatoora) rules, which require approved software. If you are not registered, you must not charge VAT on your invoices. For simple invoices and quotations, try the Invoice & Quotation maker, and always confirm current rules on the official ZATCA website, as thresholds and requirements can change.

UAE and other Gulf countries

The UAE charges 5% VAT, Bahrain 10% and Oman 5%. The same method works: multiply by 1 plus the rate to add VAT, divide to remove it. The VAT calculator has these rates built in.
