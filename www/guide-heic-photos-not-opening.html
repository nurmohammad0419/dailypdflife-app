You copy photos from your iPhone to an office computer, or try to upload a photo to a job or visa portal, and you get an error: “file type not supported”, or the file shows a blank icon with the ending .HEIC. Nothing is wrong with your photo. It's simply saved in a format that many systems still don't understand.

What is HEIC?

Since iOS 11, iPhones save photos in HEIF/HEIC format by default (the “High Efficiency” setting). A HEIC photo looks the same as a JPG but is roughly half the file size, which saves storage space on your phone. The problem is compatibility: many older Windows computers, Android phones, websites and government or company upload forms accept only JPG or PNG.

Fix 1: Convert HEIC to JPG (fastest)

  1. Open HEIC to JPG on any phone or computer.
  2. Choose your HEIC photos. You can select many at once.
  3. Pick JPG and a quality of about 90%.
  4. Tap Convert, then Download all.

The conversion happens in your browser, so your personal photos are not uploaded to anyone. If the portal also has a size limit, run the JPGs through Compress image. If it wants a PDF, use JPG to PDF.

Fix 2: Make your iPhone save JPG from now on

Go to Settings → Camera → Formats and choose Most Compatible. New photos will be saved as JPG (and videos as H.264). Photos you already took stay HEIC, so you'll still need Fix 1 for those. The trade-off is that new photos take more storage space.

Fix 3: Let the iPhone convert when transferring

Go to Settings → Photos and, under “Transfer to Mac or PC”, choose Automatic. When you copy photos to a computer with a cable, the iPhone converts them to JPG on the way. Note that this does not apply when you send photos by some apps or cloud services.

What about Windows?

Recent versions of Windows 10 and 11 can show HEIC photos after installing Microsoft's HEIF and HEVC image extensions from the Microsoft Store. That lets you view them on that computer, but websites and portals may still reject the files, so converting to JPG is usually the more reliable solution.

Sending photos by WhatsApp or email

When you send a photo through WhatsApp, it is normally converted to JPG automatically, but it is also compressed and loses quality. For documents and ID photos where sharpness matters, convert to JPG first and send as a document instead of as a photo.

Quick answers

Does converting reduce quality? At 90% JPG quality, you won't see a difference in normal use.

JPG or PNG? JPG for photos; it's smaller and accepted everywhere. PNG is bigger and best for screenshots and graphics.

Can I convert Live Photos? The still image is converted; the short video part is not included.
